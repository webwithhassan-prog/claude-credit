## 1. Case studies and income reports of calculator/tool sites (traffic, revenue, RPM, number of tools, growth timeline, what worked)

### Takeaway
The big incumbents (Calculator.net, Omni Calculator, RapidTables) still draw eight-figure monthly visits as of about 2025. Omni's decade-long, bootstrapped, ad-funded climb to 3,700+ calculators and roughly 70 staff shows that the model can scale. The one well-documented solo success is Photopea, at about $3M/yr, about 90% from ads. It is a deep, app-like free tool, not a simple calculator. Credible public revenue and RPM figures for calculator sites specifically are scarce, and the few indie data points show modest ad earnings per visitor.

### Cited Findings
**Omni Calculator (broad, multi-niche; Kraków, Poland)**
- It began as a hobby project in 2011 and launched in 2014 under founder Mateusz Mucha (a self-taught JavaScript developer). It is bootstrapped with no VC. It grew from about 20,000 monthly visits in 2016 to 18M+ in 2024, and one profile claims "30M+ monthly users." — [StorsenDigital profile](https://storsendigital.com/mateusz-mucha-omni-calculator/) (secondary profile; the 30M figure is unverified)
- It had 3,700+ calculators (some sources say 3,800+) as of about 2024-2025. — [StorsenDigital](https://storsendigital.com/mateusz-mucha-omni-calculator/); [Omni About Us](https://www.omnicalculator.com/about-us)
- An earlier interview (undated, clearly years older) described Omni with "0 VC funding, 6 million users and 900 calculators." This suggests growth came from steadily multiplying calculator count over years. — [OMG KRK interview](https://www.omgkrk.com/omni_calculator_interview/) (older data, date not confirmed)
- A Google for Publishers case study says Omni used steady ad revenue to hire an international team of about 70 employees. It monetizes with Google ad products (AdSense). — [Google for Publishers: Omni Calculator](https://www.google.com/ads/publisher/stories/omni_calculator/) (via search summary; date of story not confirmed)
- Traffic estimates vary: 17.35M monthly visits (Similarweb-sourced, cited by Ahrefs), 14.7M reach (Kochava media index), 18M+. Similarweb showed an 11.03% month-over-month traffic decline as of June 2026, which is a single-month figure and not a trend. — [Ahrefs free-tools article](https://ahrefs.com/blog/the-free-tools-seo-strategy/); [Kochava Media Index](https://media-index.kochava.com/ad_partners/omni-calculator); [Similarweb omnicalculator.com](https://www.similarweb.com/website/omnicalculator.com/)
- Omni publishes named author pages for calculator authors (e.g., the founder's profile page). This is an E-E-A-T-style attribution practice. — [Omni author page: Mateusz Mucha](https://www.omnicalculator.com/authors/mateusz-mucha)
- TechCrunch covered the launch in June 2016 ("Omni Calculator brings math to the masses"). — [TechCrunch 2016](https://techcrunch.com/2016/06/24/omni-calculator-brings-math-to-the-masses/) (older)

**Calculator.net, RapidTables, CalculatorSoup (broad incumbents)**
- Similarweb-based figures cited by Ahrefs (about 2025): calculator.net 56.12M monthly visits, omnicalculator.com 17.35M, rapidtables.com 12.95M. — [Ahrefs: The Free Tools SEO Strategy](https://ahrefs.com/blog/the-free-tools-seo-strategy/)
- Calculator.net's traffic mix, per a third-party estimator, is about 52% Google organic and about 38% direct. The same estimator gives about 633K daily unique users and about $838/day in ad revenue. The revenue figure comes from a generic "site worth" tool and is low-reliability, probably a large underestimate. — [siteworthtraffic](https://www.siteworthtraffic.com/report/calculator.net); [Similarweb calculator.net](https://www.similarweb.com/website/calculator.net/)
- I found no founder interview or income disclosure for Calculator.net, CalculatorSoup or RapidTables.

**Photopea (single deep free tool, solo founder; the best-documented ad-funded tool)**
- About $3M revenue in 2024, about 90% from advertising, run by one person (Ivan Kutskir). Annual costs were about $12.6K (hosting about $600, AI features about $12K). Revenue was $1.5M in 2023 and about $2.8M in 2024. Display ads were said to generate about $100K/month. — [BuiltPlain](https://www.builtplain.com/photopea-free-app-ad-revenue/); [Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/photopea); [Failory interview](https://www.failory.com/interview/photopea) (secondary sources; the figures conflict slightly)
- Timeline: roughly "$0 per month for 4 years" before revenue took off. — [Medium (Yuma Ueno)](https://medium.com/@yumaueno/0-per-month-for-4-years-to-200k-per-month-ivan-kutskir-who-created-a-photo-tool-photopea-869fc239d697)

**Small / indie data points (anecdotal)**
- MyPayCalculator.co.uk (a UK take-home-pay calculator, one niche) was described as a 10-month-old site with an estimated 150K visitors/month and about $7,500/yr in AdSense. — [Blog post (Goodreads mirror)](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/25154711-10-month-old-calculator-website-already-earning-7-5k-year?tab=book) (date unknown, likely pre-2024; estimated rather than owner-reported)
- Indie Hackers post: a site's AdSense revenue fell from an $800/mo peak to $200/mo in 2024 "despite the same traffic and engagement." The owner switched to direct ad sales and reached $3K/mo with 8 monthly sponsors. — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/from-200-to-3k-month-why-i-ditched-adsense-for-direct-ad-sales-with-numbers-d1098e3aae) (anecdotal; the summary does not say whether it was a tool site)
- Generic RPM benchmarks (aggregator): 10K monthly pageviews on basic AdSense is about $30-50/mo. 100K pageviews in a high-RPM niche like personal finance with header bidding is about $1,500-2,500/mo. Finance/B2B sites earn about $15-50 RPM and general content about $5-20. — [Publisher Collective / aggregator via search](https://www.publisher-collective.com/blog/adsense-revenue-calculator) (vendor-sourced benchmarks; not specific to tool pages)

### Inferences
- MyPayCalculator's figures imply roughly $4 per 1,000 visitors ($625/mo ÷ 150K). That is in line with low-RPM AdSense on short tool sessions. Tool pages typically get short sessions with few pageviews per visit, so RPM per visitor tends to be lower than on long-form content unless the niche has high advertiser value (finance, insurance, health, B2B).
- The pattern among winners is breadth built over many years (Omni took about 10 years to reach 3,700 calculators) or one exceptionally deep tool (Photopea). Neither is a quick win for a new site.
- Calculator.net's roughly 38% direct traffic suggests bookmarked, repeat-use tools build a brand moat that is less exposed to Google updates and AI Overviews.
- AdSense-only monetization looks fragile (see the IH anecdote). Winners usually move to header bidding or premium ad management, or add direct sales and subscriptions (Photopea sells premium).

### Gaps
- I found no verified revenue, RPM or income report for Calculator.net, CalculatorSoup, RapidTables, Inch Calculator or GigaCalculator.
- I found no verified 2024-2026 income reports from small or indie calculator sites on IndieHackers, X or Reddit. The search budget ran out before r/juststart and r/SEO threads could be surfaced, and those domains were not fetchable.
- There is no flipping-marketplace data (Flippa or Empire Flippers calculator listings with multiples or RPM). The search budget was exhausted.
- I found no RPM data specific to calculator pages from Mediavine, Raptive or Ezoic.

## 2. Impact of the Sept 2023 HCU, March 2024 core update, and 2024-2026 core/spam updates on tool and calculator sites

### Takeaway
I found no study that isolates calculator or tool sites as a category. Indirect evidence says that established calculator incumbents weathered 2023-2025 far better than HCU-hit content blogs: Omni had only a brief dip in 2023, and the large incumbents still showed eight-figure traffic in 2025. Meanwhile, HCU-hit blogs mostly did not recover. The March 2024 "scaled content abuse" policy is the main risk for new tool sites built from mass-generated pages, AI or template, with little unique value.

### Cited Findings
- Sistrix: OmniCalculator.com took a "slight hit" in the March 2023 core update and bounced back right after the April 2023 update. — [Sistrix Core Update March 2023](https://www.sistrix.com/blog/google-core-update-march-2023/) (2023 data, before the HCU)
- Lily Ray: of 130 sites hit hardest by the September 2023 HCU, 129 saw only further visibility decline afterward. HCU-classified sites have "mostly just been wiped off the map." — [Lily Ray LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/lily-ray-44755615_seo-helpfulcontentupdate-hcu-activity-7162899994818068481-SypY); [RankingHacks summary](https://www.rankinghacks.com/lily-ray-google-updates/)
- About 22% of heavily impacted HCU sites recovered 20% or more of lost traffic by August 2024. — [TheStacc HCU recovery study (400+ sites)](https://thestacc.com/blog/helpful-content-update-recovery/) (secondary study; methodology not reviewed)
- March 2024 core and spam updates: Google formalized a "scaled content abuse" spam policy aimed at mass-produced content made mainly to manipulate rankings, "regardless of whether humans, AI, or a mix generated it." Google said the update would cut low-quality, unoriginal results by about 40%. Reports say at least 1,446 sites received manual actions and were deindexed. The typical profile was thousands of auto-published AI articles. — [seo.ai](https://seo.ai/blog/google-march-2024-core-spam-update); [AnalogHQ](https://analoghq.ai/blog/en/google-core-update-march-2024/); [Content Ladder](https://contentladder.in/blog/scaled-content-abuse/) (secondary; the deindexing count comes from third-party tracking)
- Sistrix, August 2024 core update: some smaller content projects hurt by earlier updates (e.g., HouseFresh) appeared among the winners, and Reddit was the biggest visibility winner. — [Sistrix Core Update August 2024](https://www.sistrix.com/blog/core-update-august-2024/)
- Sistrix US 2024 losers: many previously strong sites, especially product-review sites, declined as forums (Reddit, Quora) took top rankings. — [Sistrix IndexWatch US Losers 2024](https://www.sistrix.com/blog/indexwatch-seo-losers-in-google-us-search-2024/)
- Later updates exist: a December 2025 core update ([Stan Ventures](https://www.stanventures.com/news/google-december-2025-core-update-6455/)) and a March core update (apparently 2026) that per Sistrix left "4 losers for every winner in Germany" ([SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-march-core-update-left-4-losers-for-every-winner-in-germany/571639/)). I found no calculator-specific findings for either.
- Ahrefs (about 2025-2026) describes free tools (calculators, converters, generators) as "some of the highest-traffic pages on the internet," which implies the format is still viable. — [Ahrefs: The Free Tools SEO Strategy](https://ahrefs.com/blog/the-free-tools-seo-strategy/)

### Inferences
- Tool pages appear more resilient than informational blog posts. The query is task-shaped (the user needs to compute something), and a working tool is the clearest possible "helpful content" signal. Evidence for this: calculator incumbents kept large traffic through 2023-2025, while HCU-hit blogs did not recover. This is an inference from separate data points, not a controlled comparison.
- The same updates that punished thin content would punish a new tool site that ships hundreds of near-duplicate AI-generated calculator pages ("X to Y converter" for every pair) with templated boilerplate text. The scaled-content-abuse policy targets the practice, not the tooling, so quantity without distinct value is the risk.
- Reddit and forum gains since 2024 are mostly on discussion-type queries. That matters less for "do the calculation" queries, but it crowds out explanatory content around tools.

### Gaps
- I found no Sistrix, Semrush or Ahrefs dataset tracking a cohort of calculator or tool domains through the Sept 2023 HCU, March 2024 core update, or 2025-2026 core updates.
- I found no Glenn Gabe or Lily Ray commentary specifically on calculator or tool sites. The searches returned only general HCU and core update material.
- I did not verify Omni, Calculator.net or RapidTables visibility charts for 2024-2026. Sistrix pages could not be fetched.

## 3. Impact of Google AI Overviews / AI Mode and LLM search on calculator queries: which still get clicks vs. are answered directly? CTR studies 2025-2026

### Takeaway
Across studies, CTR falls roughly 35-61% when an AI Overview appears: Ahrefs position 1 −34.5% (Apr 2025) and −58% (Dec 2025); Seer organic −61%. Zero-click searches reached about 68% in early 2026. Queries with a single universal answer are the ones being absorbed: unit conversions, "what is X% of Y," definitions. Queries that need personal inputs still send clicks, and Seer found CTR on non-AIO queries actually rose in 2025. The newest threat is Google's Gemini 3 "generative UI" (Nov 2025), which builds custom interactive calculators, such as mortgage and loan comparisons, inside AI Mode and later AI Overviews. That targets even the personalized-calculator category.

### Cited Findings
**CTR studies**
- Seer Interactive (Sept 2025 update): on queries with AI Overviews, organic CTR fell 61% (1.76% to 0.61%) and paid CTR fell 68% (19.7% to 6.34%). Dataset: 3,119 informational queries, 42 organizations, 25.1M organic and 1.1M paid impressions, June 2024 to Sept 2025. Brands cited in the AIO got 35% higher organic CTR and 91% higher paid CTR than non-cited brands on the same queries. — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-ai-overviews-drive-drop-organic-paid-ctr-464212); [Seer Sept 2025 update](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/aio-impact-on-google-ctr-september-2025-update)
- Seer 2026 update (published April 2026; 53 brands, 5.47M tracked queries, 2.43B organic impressions; Jan 2025 to Feb 2026 actuals): on queries without an AIO, organic CTR rose from 2.93% (Jan 2025) to 3.97% (Dec 2025). Seer's hypothesis is a selection effect: AIO captures low-engagement, answer-seeking queries, and the non-AIO pool skews toward users who will click and toward questions with "not a universal answer," which Seer calls an "opportunity to differentiate with value." — [Seer 2026 update](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/aio-impact-on-google-ctr-2026-update); SEJ headline: ["AI Overview CTR Fell 61%, But Clicks Didn't Collapse"](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-overview-ctr-fell-61-but-clicks-didnt-collapse/572993/)
- Ahrefs: in April 2025 an AIO was associated with a 34.5% lower CTR for the position-1 result. December 2025 data (300,000 keywords) showed a 58% reduction. — [Ahrefs (Apr 2025)](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks/); [Ahrefs update](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update); [MediaNama, Feb 2026](https://www.medianama.com/2026/02/223-google-ai-overviews-click-through-rates-58-study/)
- Aggregator restatement: CTR drops about 58% at position 1, 51% at position 2 and 46% at position 3. Position 1 goes from about 39.8% to about 16.7% CTR when an AIO shows. Informational queries trigger AIOs about 90% of the time, with CTR down 47-61%. — [Omnibound (aggregator)](https://www.omnibound.ai/blog/zero-click-search-statistics)
- Pew Research (July 22, 2025; browsing data from 900 US adults, March 2025): users clicked a traditional result on 8% of visits with an AI summary vs 15% without. They clicked a link inside the AI summary 1% of the time. 58% of respondents had at least one search that produced an AI summary. — [Pew Research](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/)
- SparkToro 2026 (Similarweb panel, Jan-Apr 2026): 68.01% of Google searches ended with no click, so fewer than one-third send any click. The share was about 45% in 2016, about 49% in 2019 and 60.45% in 2024 (Datos data). The share of traffic Google sent to the tracked websites fell about 8 points (about 22%) from June 2025 to May 2026. — [SparkToro](https://sparktoro.com/blog/in-2026-less-than-one-third-of-google-searches-still-send-a-click/)
- Similarweb (aggregator): zero-click searches rose from 56% to 69% between May 2024 and May 2025. Separately, US referrals from GenAI platforms fell about 15% from Oct 2025 to Jan 2026. — [Omnibound (aggregator)](https://www.omnibound.ai/blog/zero-click-search-statistics)

**Where AIOs appear**
- Semrush: in January 2025, 91.3% of AIO-triggering queries were informational. By October 2025 that fell to 57.1%, with the rest commercial, transactional or navigational. AIO presence on navigational queries rose from under 1% to over 10%. SERPs showing both ads and AIOs grew over 394% in 2025. — [Semrush AIO commercial study](https://www.semrush.com/blog/ai-overviews-commercial-search-study/); [Semrush SERPs with Ads + AIO](https://www.semrush.com/blog/serps-with-ads-and-ai-overviews/). (Note: the search summary also gave conflicting shares — commercial 8.69%, transactional 1.76%, navigational 1.43% — that do not reconcile with the 42.9% figure. They probably measure something different, such as the share of each intent's keywords, and should be checked.)
- Unit conversions (e.g., "how many cups in a gallon") are a standard example of zero-click queries that Google answers directly on the SERP. — [Click-Vision zero-click stats](https://click-vision.com/zero-click-search-statistics)
- Guides observe that action-oriented "find a tool or calculator" queries are less likely to show an AIO than explanatory ones. — [Thrive Agency AIO guide](https://thriveagency.com/ai-search-optimization/ai-overviews/) (practitioner opinion, no dataset)

**Generative UI (threat to interactive calculators)**
- Gemini 3 launch (Nov 18, 2025): when an interactive tool would help, Gemini 3 in AI Mode "code[s] custom simulations or tools in real-time." Google's example is a custom-built interactive loan calculator for comparing two mortgage options, with inputs for timeline, rate, down payment and price. — [Google blog: Gemini 3 in Search/AI Mode](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/gemini-3-search-ai-mode/); [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/11/18/gemini-3-launch/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-launches-gemini-3-with-generative-ui-for-dynamic-search-experiences/)
- Google later expanded generative UI "beyond AI Mode into AI Overviews." — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-expands-generative-ui-beyond-ai-mode-into-ai-overviews/586452/) (exact date not confirmed; probably 2026)

### Inferences
- **Most at risk (answered in the SERP, AIO or Gemini):** single-answer queries, meaning unit and currency conversions, basic percentages, simple date and age math, tip and discount math, definitions, and "formula for X." Google's native calculator and converter widgets already served these before AIOs.
- **Still clicking (for now):** tools that need several personal inputs, iteration, and outputs users want to keep, compare or trust. Examples: amortization schedules, tax and paycheck calculators with current-year jurisdiction tables, retirement or FIRE projections, construction and material estimators, health or dosing tools with methodology, and file-processing tools (PDF, image) that AI answers cannot perform. Seer's rising non-AIO CTR supports the idea that searchers without a "universal answer" still click.
- The Gemini 3 generative UI example is a mortgage/loan calculator, one of the highest-value calculator niches. Expect finance calculators to face growing in-SERP competition from 2026 on. Moats must come from what a disposable in-answer tool lacks: trusted, sourced and maintained data (tax brackets, rates, local rules), saved and shareable scenarios, exports, embeds, depth of options, and brand or repeat use (Calculator.net's roughly 38% direct traffic).
- Being cited in AIOs matters (Seer: +35% organic CTR for cited brands). Clear methodology and explanatory text on tool pages may increase citation odds, but I found no direct evidence for tool pages.
- LLM referrals (ChatGPT, Perplexity) are too small and too volatile (−15% Oct 2025 to Jan 2026 per Similarweb, via aggregator) to replace Google for an ad-funded tool site.

### Gaps
- I found no study measuring AIO trigger rates or CTR for calculator or tool keywords specifically (e.g., "mortgage calculator" vs "cm to inches").
- I found no data on how often AI Mode or AIO actually shows generative-UI calculators, or on the traffic impact for calculator sites since Nov 2025.
- I found no data on ChatGPT or Perplexity referral volumes to calculator sites.

## 4. Product features that differentiate winning tool pages

### Takeaway
I found almost no hard, tool-specific evidence (A/B or ranking studies) on which features win. The documented practices are: supporting content and FAQs under the tool (Ahrefs), author attribution (Omni), embed codes built before launch (link-building guides), and "differentiate with value" on queries without a universal answer (Seer). FAQ rich results no longer show for most sites, so FAQ schema is not a SERP feature lever.

### Cited Findings
- Ahrefs recommends adding FAQs beneath calculators that answer specific questions so the page can rank for those queries. It also notes that anyone can now build a calculator "in an afternoon" with ChatGPT-class tools, so "the bottleneck is no longer engineering, it's knowing which tool to build." — [Ahrefs: The Free Tools SEO Strategy](https://ahrefs.com/blog/the-free-tools-seo-strategy/)
- Omni Calculator attributes calculators to named authors with profile pages. — [Omni author page](https://www.omnicalculator.com/authors/mateusz-mucha)
- A link-building guide says tools should ship with a copy-paste embed snippet that works on WordPress, Shopify, Wix and custom sites: "Build the embed code before launch." — [Link Building Journal](https://linkbuildingjournal.co.uk/interactive-calculators-100-links/) (practitioner guide)
- Seer: queries without AIO skew toward users who will click and toward questions with no universal answer, an "opportunity to differentiate with value." — [Seer 2026 update](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/aio-impact-on-google-ctr-2026-update)
- Google's generative UI builds interactive comparison calculators (two mortgage options) in the SERP. — [Google blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/gemini-3-search-ai-mode/)
- (Prior knowledge; not re-verified this session because Google domains were blocked) In August 2023 Google limited FAQ rich results to "well-known, authoritative government and health websites" and cut back HowTo rich results. — [Google Search Central blog, Aug 2023](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/08/howto-faq-changes)

### Inferences
- Features likely to matter now, inferred from the AI-competition analysis above and not from direct studies:
  1. **Current, sourced data and methodology.** Current-year tax tables and regional rules, a visible formula and sources, and a "last updated" date. This is hard for a disposable AI tool to match, and it supports citation in AIOs.
  2. **Persistence and sharing.** Shareable result URLs, saved scenarios, side-by-side comparisons.
  3. **Rich outputs.** Amortization tables, charts, CSV/PDF export, printable results.
  4. **Speed and mobile UX.** Mobile-first, no signup. No source found this session, but this is standard Core Web Vitals and page-experience practice.
  5. **Explanatory content.** Worked examples and an FAQ below the tool (Ahrefs).
  6. **An embed code**, for link acquisition (see section 5).
  7. **Clusters of related tools** with internal links, rather than one-off pages.
- Schema: use WebApplication/SoftwareApplication plus BreadcrumbList as hygiene. Do not count on FAQ rich results (restricted since 2023).
- Multilingual expansion is a proven growth lever for incumbents (Omni runs localized versions). I did not verify that this session, so treat it as unconfirmed.

### Gaps
- I found no study linking specific tool features (exports, shareable URLs, charts) to rankings, CTR or RPM.
- I found no evidence on whether SoftwareApplication or WebApplication schema produces rich results or ranking benefits for calculator pages in 2024-2026.
- I did not verify multilingual impact data or Core Web Vitals effects for tool sites.

## 5. Link-building strategies that work for tool sites

### Takeaway
Free tools are naturally linkable assets, and embeddable calculators can build links at scale (Redfin is the cited example). But Google has long said that keyword-rich, hidden or low-quality links inside distributed widgets violate its guidelines and should be nofollow. Embeds should carry branded, visible, optional attribution and not be treated as a PageRank farm.

### Cited Findings
- Google's position (Matt Cutts 2013; Google Search Central reminder Sept 2016): widget links placed without the embedding webmaster's editorial control, especially keyword-rich, hidden or low-quality anchors, violate the guidelines. Google recommends `rel="nofollow"` on widget links and may take manual action. Not all widget links are banned, only keyword-rich, hidden or low-quality ones. — [Google Search Central Blog: A reminder about widget links (2016)](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2016/09/a-reminder-about-widget-links); [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-reminds-webmasters-widget-links-webmaster-guidelines-258393); [SEJ: Matt Cutts recommends nofollowing widget links](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/matt-cutts-recommends-nofollowing-links-on-embedded-widgets/67212/) (older guidance, still referenced in Google's spam policies)
- Redfin's linkable assets, including embeddable calculators, reportedly generate dozens of backlinks a month. One page has earned 5.7K backlinks from 2.1K referring domains. — [Link Building Journal](https://linkbuildingjournal.co.uk/interactive-calculators-100-links/) (practitioner source; the figures are Ahrefs-style estimates)
- The same guide argues that calculators computing "public numbers" (stats that others cite) earn durable links inside evergreen articles. Personal-answer tools tend to get resource-list links that disappear when lists are refreshed. — [Link Building Journal](https://linkbuildingjournal.co.uk/interactive-calculators-100-links/) (opinion)
- Ahrefs frames free tools as a core way to earn traffic and links. — [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/the-free-tools-seo-strategy/)
- Reddit as a channel (aggregator claim): Reddit has about 80M weekly search users, and Reddit Answers queries grew 1,400% in 2025. — [Replymer (aggregator)](https://replymer.com/blog/reddit-seo-complete-guide-2026) (low-reliability source)

### Inferences
- The strategy best supported by this evidence:
  1. Build calculators that produce **citable data or public numbers** (e.g., cost-of-X indexes, benchmark tables) that bloggers and journalists reference.
  2. Offer an **embed code** with a visible branded attribution link. Keep anchors branded, not keyword-stuffed, and consider nofollow to stay inside Google's widget guidance. The value then shifts toward referral traffic and brand, and the SEO lift is less certain.
  3. Answer relevant Reddit and Quora questions where the tool genuinely solves the problem, since forums gained visibility in 2024.
  4. Launch posts (Product Hunt, Hacker News "Show HN," niche communities) for early links. I found no 2024-2026 data on Product Hunt's effect for tool sites.

### Gaps
- I found no 2024-2026 case study quantifying links earned by an indie calculator site's embed program.
- I found no data on how effective directories or Product Hunt launches are for tool-site SEO.
- I found no evidence on whether Google currently discounts widget links in bulk algorithmically or only acts manually.

## 6. Site architecture: broad multi-niche tool site vs. focused niche tool site for a new domain (2025-2026)

### Takeaway
The biggest calculator winners are broad (Calculator.net, Omni, RapidTables), but they are decade-old domains with deep link profiles. For a new domain, the evidence (Google's leaked site-focus metrics, topical-authority practice, and how Omni and MyPayCalculator grew) favors starting with a tightly focused cluster of related tools, then expanding into adjacent niches.

### Cited Findings
- The May 2024 Google Content Warehouse API leak showed `siteFocusScore` (how concentrated a site is on one topic) and `siteRadius` (how far a page's topic deviates from the site's core theme), stored in the QualityNsrNsrData module linked to "Normalized Site Rank," along with `site2vecEmbeddingEncoded`. — [Hobo Web: Topical Authority, Site Radius & Site Focus Score](https://www.hobo-web.co.uk/topical-authority/); [Wikipedia: 2024 Google Search documentation leak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Google_Search_documentation_leak). (The leak shows these attributes exist, not how they are weighted in ranking.)
- Practitioners cite Ahrefs data that 90.63% of pages get zero Google organic traffic. They argue that new, low-authority sites rank through focused topical depth and interlinking. — [Keyword Insights / aggregator summary](https://www.keywordinsights.ai/blog/how-to-build-topical-authority-in-seo/); Kevin Indig on measuring topical authority: [Growth Memo](https://www.growth-memo.com/p/how-to-measure-topical-authority) (opinion and practitioner guidance)
- Broad incumbents' scale: calculator.net about 56M, Omni about 17M, RapidTables about 13M monthly visits (about 2025). — [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/the-free-tools-seo-strategy/)
- Omni went from about 900 calculators to 3,700+ over many years (2014 to about 2024) while running with a large team (about 70 people). — [OMG KRK](https://www.omgkrk.com/omni_calculator_interview/); [StorsenDigital](https://storsendigital.com/mateusz-mucha-omni-calculator/); [Google for Publishers](https://www.google.com/ads/publisher/stories/omni_calculator/)
- A single-niche calculator site (UK take-home pay) reportedly reached about 150K visitors/month within 10 months. — [Blog post](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/25154711-10-month-old-calculator-website-already-earning-7-5k-year?tab=book) (anecdotal, date unknown)

### Inferences
- For a new domain in 2025-2026, the recommended shape is a **niche-first hub**: one high-value vertical (e.g., personal finance for one country, construction and home improvement, fitness and nutrition, or a professional or B2B niche). Build 20-50 deeply built, interlinked tools plus supporting guides. Expand to adjacent clusters only once the core cluster ranks. This lines up with the leaked focus signals and with a small team's realistic output.
- Pick verticals for (a) high advertiser value (RPM), (b) personal, multi-input calculations that AI Overviews and generic widgets do not fully answer, and (c) data that needs maintenance (annual tax tables, local rules) to create a freshness moat. Watch for Google's generative UI moving into finance calculators.
- A broad "everything calculator" site on a new domain would compete head-on with Calculator.net and Omni. Their link equity and brand/direct traffic (about 38% direct for Calculator.net) are hard to overcome, and broad thin coverage risks scaled-content-abuse signals.
- Diversify revenue beyond AdSense early: premium ad management once traffic qualifies, direct sponsors (the IH anecdote), and premium features, exports or APIs (the Photopea model). Ad-only tool sites face shrinking Google clicks (68% zero-click in 2026).

### Gaps
- I found no controlled or cohort evidence comparing growth of niche vs broad tool sites launched in 2024-2026.
- I found no public data on how new calculator domains fared after the 2025-2026 core updates.
- The ranking weight of siteFocusScore and siteRadius is unknown. The leak confirms only that the attributes exist.
