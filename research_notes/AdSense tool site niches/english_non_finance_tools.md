## Q1: Which tool ideas have strong CPC and weak SERP competition, and how do YMYL competitors handle disclaimers?

### Takeaway
The best combination of high-CPC verticals and weak SERPs is in **legal and benefits calculators**: alimony, divorce cost, DUI cost, child support, workers' comp settlement, SSDI back pay, VA disability, ACA subsidy, IRMAA and pet insurance. Their SERPs are mostly law-firm lead-gen pages and small or new exact-match tool sites, with few big publishers. The most expensive home-improvement ideas that are still open are **heat pump vs furnace** and **window replacement cost**. **Life, car, homeowners insurance and moving-cost** head terms are locked up by big brands (MoneyGeek, NerdWallet, Progressive, Allstate, USNews, Insurify, van lines).

### Inferences
- **Top tier (high CPC vertical plus weak SERP)**: alimony by state, divorce cost by state, DUI cost by state, child support by state, workers' comp settlement by state, SSDI back pay, the ACA subsidy / 400% cliff calculator, IRMAA, and heat pump vs furnace by state/climate. Each has a legal, insurance, health-insurance or home-improvement advertiser pool, and each SERP is mostly thin or new sites and law-firm pages.
- **Second tier (winnable with a better tool or data)**: window replacement cost, pet insurance by breed, VA disability combined rating with dependents, EV vs gas by state, solar payback by state/utility (EnergySage dominates the head term), and kitchen remodel by region.
- **Avoid as head terms**: life insurance needs, car insurance cost, homeowners insurance cost and moving cost are brand-dominated. The possible exception is narrow state or scenario long tails, such as a Florida home insurance estimator or "SR-22 insurance cost [state]". The cost-of-employee and ROAS SERPs are crowded with funded SaaS lead magnets.
- **CPC caveat**: vertical CPCs such as legal $8.58 to $9.87 or the $54.91 insurance root are advertiser bids for transactional terms. Informational "calculator" queries usually attract lower bids, and AdSense pays the publisher only a share of the click. Settlement and legal-cost pages probably draw lawyer advertisers, but I found no source quantifying AdSense RPM specifically for calculator pages.
- **YMYL**: settlement, SSDI, VA, child support and alimony tools are "Your Money or Your Life" content. The surviving small competitors show the minimum standard: cite the statute or official schedule, show the working, label the result "estimate / not legal advice", date-stamp the formula year, and name who built or reviewed it. Law firms win on E-E-A-T. A new site can partly offset that with transparent formulas and primary-source citations to state guidelines, SSA and VA rate tables, or CMS.

## Q2: Which ideas are state-specific in the US, and is programmatic state-page SEO still safe after the 2024-2026 scaled-content and helpful-content changes?

### Takeaway
Child support, alimony, divorce cost, DUI cost, workers' comp, car and home insurance, and solar or heat-pump savings all vary legally or economically by state. Each supports 50+ genuinely different pages. SERPs in Sept 2026 still reward this: statecalc.com, supportbystate.com, alimonycalcbystate.com, workcompcalc.com and flhomeinsuranceestimate.com all rank. Google's scaled content abuse policy, however, explicitly targets templated pages that only swap location names. State pages are safe only when each one runs that state's actual formula or data.

### Cited Findings
- State-specific mechanics confirmed:
  - Child support: each state follows income shares (41 states), percentage of income, or Melson — [statecalc guide](https://statecalc.com/blog/child-support-guide/). Official state calculators exist for CA, TX, OH, MN, TN, WA, NH and others — [CA](https://childsupport.ca.gov/guideline-calculator/), [TN](https://www.tn.gov/humanservices/for-families/child-support-services/child-support-guidelines/child-support-calculator-and-worksheet-1.html), [WA](https://fortress.wa.gov/dshs/dcs/SSGen/Home/QuickEstimator)
  - Alimony: formulas in IL, NY and TX, discretion elsewhere — [divorce.law](https://divorce.law/guides/alimony-calculator-guide/)
  - Workers' comp: "benefits and settlements vary significantly by state and are governed by complex statutory frameworks". California uses impairment rating, age, occupation and apportionment — [scworkerscomp CA calculator](https://www.scworkerscomp.com/calculators/california-workers-comp-settlement-calculator); [workcompcalc](https://workcompcalc.com/workers-comp-settlement-calculator/)
  - DUI: first DUI about $15,000 to $20,000 in CA vs $12,000 to $18,000 in FL. State-specific tools such as the Iowa DUI Cost Calculator exist — [bestlawyersinunitedstates](https://bestlawyersinunitedstates.com/dui-cost-calculator/); [O'Flaherty Law](https://www.oflaherty-law.com/calculators/iowa-dui-cost-calculator)
  - Car insurance: the same driver pays $97/month in Vermont vs $243 in Florida. Credit-based pricing is allowed in some states only — [statecalc car insurance](https://statecalc.com/car-insurance/); [MoneyGeek](https://www.moneygeek.com/insurance/auto/car-insurance-estimate-calculator/) (search summary)
  - Heat pump vs furnace: depends on local gas price, electricity rate and climate. Example: Georgia saves $344/year with a 10 HSPF heat pump, while replacing a 95% gas furnace in Zone 6 "might never break even without incentives" — [PickHVAC](https://www.pickhvac.com/hvac/furnace-vs-heat-pump-cost/)
  - EV vs gas: charging costs 40 to 65% less than fuel depending on state electricity rates — [Coltura](https://coltura.org/ev-vs-gas-calculator/)
- Programmatic state sites ranking now (Sept 2026): statecalc.com appears for child support, alimony and car insurance "by state" queries. supportbystate.com, childsupportcalculator.io, alimonycalcbystate.com and workcompcalc.com ("All 50 States") rank for their queries — [statecalc](https://statecalc.com/alimony/); [supportbystate](https://supportbystate.com/); [workcompcalc](https://workcompcalc.com/workers-comp-settlement-calculator/). I could not tell how old these sites are or how durable their rankings will be.
- Google policy: the scaled content abuse policy was introduced in March 2024. It is defined as generating many pages "with little to no unique value, primarily to manipulate search rankings". Programmatic content is not banned in itself; it becomes a violation when pages are thin and exist mainly to rank. The policy is enforced algorithmically (demotion during core and spam updates) and by manual actions in Search Console — [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/scaled-content-abuse-google-march-update-ai-pages-decimated); [Patrick Stox](https://patrickstox.com/programmatic-seo/risks/scaled-content-abuse/); [Breakline](https://www.breaklineagency.com/guide-to-googles-scaled-content-abuse/) (search summary; the primary Google doc at developers.google.com was blocked)
- Patterns flagged in the March 2026 enforcement wave: sites publishing 50 to 500 AI articles per day with no editorial review; **"data-template pages that swap location names... into identical page structures"**; sites with only stock or AI images; and AI author personas with no external presence — [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/scaled-content-abuse-google-march-update-ai-pages-decimated); [Easton](https://eastondev.com/blog/en/posts/media/20260326-programmatic-seo-guide-2025/) (secondary SEO commentary, not Google's own words)
- What stays allowed: programmatic pages where "each generated page provides real, differentiated value, such as accurate product data or genuinely useful local information" — [Gautam Khorana](https://gautamkhorana.com/blog/scaled-content-abuse-programmatic-seo/); [Layer3 Labs](https://www.layer3labs.io/guides/scaled-content-abuse)

### Inferences
- **Safe pattern**: each state page runs that state's actual rule set, for example a child-support schedule table, a workers' comp maximum weekly rate and PPD schedule, DUI fines plus interlock and SR-22 requirements, or a state utility rate. It cites the statute or agency, shows a worked example, and carries a "last updated for [year] guidelines" stamp. These pages are functionally different, not just templated.
- **Unsafe pattern**: 50 pages that share one generic formula and only swap "{State}" into the H1 and intro. That is the exact pattern named in the 2026 enforcement commentary.
- **Where state pages add real value**: child support and workers' comp have the strongest statutory differences. Next come alimony (only a few formula states, so non-formula states need an honest factor-based explainer), DUI cost, and heat pump or EV savings (utility and fuel price data). Car and homeowners insurance vary by state too, but brand-dominated SERPs make the state pages harder to win, Florida home insurance aside.
- Official state calculators (CA, TX, OH, MN and others) will often outrank a new site for "[state] child support calculator". The gap a new site can fill is multi-scenario comparison, plain-English explanation, shared-custody what-ifs, and states with clunky or missing official tools. This is inferred; the individual state SERPs were not checked.

### Gaps
- I could not access Google's primary spam-policy text or official statements (blocked), so the policy description relies on secondary SEO sources.
- No data on which of the ranking programmatic state sites were hit or spared in the March 2026 update, and no domain ages or traffic trends (Semrush and Ahrefs blocked).
- Per-state search volume (for example "texas child support calculator" vs "ohio...") was not obtained.

## Q3: Which tool types are resilient to Google AI Overviews and generative UI?

### Takeaway
AI Overviews now reach commercial and transactional queries as well as informational ones. In 2026 Google also began rendering **interactive tools inside AI Mode and AI Overviews**, such as a loan calculator and a character counter widget. Simple one-formula converters (salary to hourly, BTU, GPA, ROAS, overtime) are therefore the most exposed. The more resilient tools are multi-input, jurisdiction-specific or proprietary-data tools where users need a documented, citable, printable result: state child support and workers' comp, VA combined rating with dependents, IRMAA and ACA cliff modeling, and cost estimators built on real local price data.

### Cited Findings
- Semrush AI Overviews study (10M+ keywords): the share of AIO-triggering queries with informational intent fell from 91.3% (Jan 2025) to 57.1% (Oct 2025). Since October 2024, the share of commercial queries triggering AIO rose from 8.15% to 18.57%, transactional from 1.98% to 13.94%, and navigational from 0.84% to 10.33% — [Semrush AI Overviews study](https://www.semrush.com/blog/semrush-ai-overviews-study/); [Semrush commercial-intent study](https://www.semrush.com/blog/ai-overviews-commercial-search-study/) (search summary; fetch blocked)
- Google AI Overviews "surged in 2025, then pulled back" — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-ai-overviews-surge-pullback-data-466314) (headline only). One summary gives AIO presence as 13% of all queries — [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/ai-overviews-13-percent-queries-seo-traffic-impact). That prevalence figure likely differs by dataset and date.
- CTR impact: Ahrefs found AIOs reduce position-1 CTR by 58%. An April 2025 study found a 34.5% reduction. Organic CTR on AIO keywords fell from 1.76% (June 2024) to 0.61% (Sept 2025), and another source puts the drop at 61% — [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update/); [Dataslayer](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/google-ai-overviews-the-end-of-traditional-ctr-and-how-to-adapt-in-2025) (search summary; the figures vary by study)
- Transactional, branded and local-intent queries are more resilient; AIOs show on under 5% of branded SERPs. Commentators recommend "original data, tools, calculators, and interactive demos that AI cannot replicate easily" — [Dataslayer](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/google-ai-overviews-the-end-of-traditional-ctr-and-how-to-adapt-in-2025); [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/ai-overviews-13-percent-queries-seo-traffic-impact) (search summary)
- **Generative UI**: Google's generative UI builds "custom visual layouts, interactive tools, and simulations on the fly". It launched in AI Mode (English, globally) and "has started rolling out in AI Overviews". Examples include an interactive loan or mortgage calculator in AI Mode that compares two options. At I/O 2026, Google said generative UI would reach everyone in Search that summer. SEJ comments: "sites built around calculators and other interactive tools could see more overlap as this expands" — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-expands-generative-ui-beyond-ai-mode-into-ai-overviews/586452/); [Google blog, Gemini 3 in Search](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/gemini-3-search-ai-mode/) (search summary; fetch blocked)
- **Tool widget inside AI Overviews**: for [character counter] queries, Google AI Overviews now load an "interactive Character Counter" widget. SERoundtable: "websites that offer character counting service features are in trouble". Published about 3 weeks before 2026-09-25, so roughly early Sept 2026 — [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-ai-overviews-character-counter-42026.html) (search summary)
- Brands cited inside AI Overviews earn 35% more organic clicks and 91% more paid clicks than uncited brands on the same queries — [Dataslayer](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/google-ai-overviews-the-end-of-traditional-ctr-and-how-to-adapt-in-2025) (search summary; original source not verified)

### Inferences
- **Most exposed** (single formula, few inputs, no jurisdiction data): salary to hourly, overtime pay, PTO accrual, GPA, HVAC BTU by room size, break-even ROAS, basic LTV/CAC, solar payback as simple division, and life-insurance DIME math. Google has already shown it will render simple utilities (character counter) and loan calculators inline, and Gemini can generate such tools on the fly.
- **More resilient**:
  1. Tools that need **jurisdiction-specific statutory tables** that change every year: state child support schedules, workers' comp max rates and body-part schedules, DUI penalty stacks by state, IRMAA brackets, ACA FPL tables and the 400% cliff, and VA compensation tables with dependents and bilateral factor.
  2. Tools built on **proprietary or local price data**: MoneyGeek's 38k-quote dataset and VetReceipt's vet bills show the model, as do window, roof and remodel costs by ZIP or region, and heat pump savings by utility rate and climate zone.
  3. **Multi-scenario planners** where users iterate and save or print results: divorce cost scenarios, alimony plus child support combined, SSDI back-pay timeline, Medicare cost projection.
  4. **Outputs people bring to a lawyer, adjuster or contractor**: worksheets and PDFs.
  These are also the YMYL-sensitive ones, where Google may be more cautious about generating answers. That is unverified.
- Dated and newsworthy formula changes (2026 ACA cliff, 2026 IRMAA brackets, annual VA COLA) create freshness demand that thin AI answers may get wrong, and they give a site a reason to earn citations or links.
- The flip side: tools whose value is a single number that Gemini can compute are at risk even if they rank #1 today. Sites that depend on them should expect CTR decay over 2026-2027.

### Gaps
- No study was found that measures AIO trigger rates specifically for "calculator" queries or by vertical (legal, health insurance, home improvement). Semrush's by-industry breakdown was not accessible.
- It is not confirmed which query types generative UI actually covers in AI Overviews, beyond loan/mortgage and character-counter examples, or whether Google suppresses it for YMYL legal and medical calculators.
- No first-hand traffic data from calculator-site owners (Reddit, IndieHackers) on AIO losses in 2025-2026 was obtained.
