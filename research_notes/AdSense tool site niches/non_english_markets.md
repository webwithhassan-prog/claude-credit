# Non-English Language Markets for a Free Tools/Calculator Site (AdSense), 2025-2026

Research date: 2026-09-25. Method caveats for the report writer:
- WebFetch was EGRESS_BLOCKED on every domain tried: support.google.com, google.com, developers.google.com, searchenginejournal.com, seroundtable.com, ybierling.com, finanzfacts.de, global-fin-info.com, consentmanager.net and omgkrk.com. Almost every finding below therefore comes from WebSearch result summaries and SERP listings, not from reading full pages.
- The session-wide WebSearch budget (200 calls, shared with other researchers) ran out partway through. Swedish/Danish/Finnish, Swiss and Belgian specifics, and keyword-volume data were not researched (see Gaps).
- The SERP observations come from a US-based search tool. They approximate local Google.de/.fr/.co.jp/etc. rankings but are not identical to them. Their purpose is to show who competes, not exact positions.
- Country RPM figures come mostly from aggregator blogs or from YouTube data. They have low reliability and are flagged where they appear.

## Q1. Which language markets combine high AdSense RPM with lower tool-site competition?

### Takeaway
German, Japanese, French, Nordic (Norwegian) and Swiss audiences sit at the top of the non-English RPM tables. The RPM evidence is weak: it comes from aggregators, anecdotes and YouTube data. The web-display anecdotes for local-language pages are much lower than English/US: German €3-7 RPM; older single-site data had German and French below $0.5 and Japanese about $0.2. GDPR consent also drags down EU yield. The claim that non-English markets have "far less competition" does not hold for head calculator terms in 2026. In every market checked (DE, FR, NL, PL, NO, IT, ES, JP, BR, KR), 5-9 dedicated calculator sites with "2026" titles already compete for the core salary and tax calculators, alongside HR/payroll SaaS, law firms, notaries, banks and official government simulators. The opportunity is in newly changed rules and in long-tail regional variants, not in head terms. Korea is a poor fit because Naver controls about 63% of search.

### Inferences
- **Suggested ranking for a new AdSense calculator site** (inference from the evidence above, not a measured result):
  1. **German (DE, plus AT/CH)**: high purchasing power, many rules that differ by federal state (Bundesland), and several 2026 rule changes. Crowded with Rechner portals, and GDPR consent reduces yield.
  2. **Japanese**: good anecdotal RPM, heavy yearly tax-rule churn, and a language barrier that keeps foreign competitors out. Needs native-quality Japanese and YMYL trust signals.
  3. **French (FR, plus BE/CH/CA)**: many regulated formulas (notary fees, severance, PASS-linked thresholds). SERPs mix law firms with small simulators.
  4. **Italian**: complex, yearly-changing welfare rules (ISEE, Assegno Unico). SERPs are full of small sites plus INPS.
  5. **Norwegian (Nordics)**: highest aggregator RPM but a small market, and several kalkulator portals already exist.
- **Deprioritize**:
  - Korean: Google SEO reaches less than 30% of searches.
  - Arabic/Gulf: only YouTube RPM data was found; site-level evidence is thin.
  - Polish and LatAm Spanish/Portuguese: large volume but lower RPM.
  - Spain: no reliable RPM figure was found.
- Head terms ("Brutto Netto Rechner", "bruto netto", "手取り 計算", "kalkulator wynagrodzeń") are contested everywhere by job boards and HR SaaS (pracuj.pl, aplikuj.pl, Randstad, talent.com, Nmbrs, Youforce) and by exact-match-domain portals. "Low competition" should be read as "fewer players than English", not "open".
- **Biggest uncertainty for the business case: the size of the RPM premium.** Rich-country traffic does not mean that *local-language pages* earn US-level RPM. The web anecdotes (German €3-7, and older data with German/French/Japanese below $0.5) suggest a smaller advertiser pool per language. Expect well below US English finance RPMs unless the page targets high-CPC local intent (mortgage, property purchase costs, insurance), where local banks and brokers bid. Validate with a small pilot before committing.
- GDPR/TCF consent loss (about 30% in one anecdote) cuts effective RPM in EU markets. This does not apply to Japan, Switzerland (non-EU, but it has its own FADP) or Norway (EEA, so GDPR does apply).

### Gaps
- No trustworthy, current (2025-2026) web-AdSense page RPM by country or language from a primary source (Google does not publish one). Every figure above is aggregator, anecdotal or YouTube data.
- No Polish, Swedish, Danish, Finnish, Dutch, Italian or Korean web RPM figures were found.
- Keyword search volumes and CPCs for the native-language terms were not obtained (no keyword-tool access, and the search budget ran out).
- Japan's search engine share (Google vs Yahoo! JAPAN, which uses Google's engine) was not verified in this run.

## Q2. High-CPC calculator types per market; dominated vs. gap niches

### Takeaway
Mortgage and loan calculators, which carry the highest advertiser CPC, are held by banks and brokers in NL, and by portal and affiliate sites for Japan's furusato nozei. Salary calculators are held by HR/payroll SaaS and job boards. The more open niches are calculators for rules that changed in 2026. Their SERPs consist mostly of small calculator sites: German Aktivrente, Grunderwerbsteuer (property transfer tax) and Elterngeld (parental allowance); French rupture conventionnelle (severance) and frais de notaire (notary fees); Italian ISEE and Assegno Unico; Japanese 年収の壁 (income threshold) and 手取り (take-home pay) after the 2026 changes; Brazilian rescisão (severance) after Lei 15.270/2025.

## Q3. Yearly-changing government-rule calculators (benefits, tax, pension, notary, severance, parental leave)

### Takeaway
Yes. Every major market has several rule changes dated 2026 that create fresh "…2026" search demand each year. However, SERPs already fill with small sites putting "2026" in their titles within months. The durable edge comes from updating fast and accurately and covering regional or edge cases, not from being first to cover a topic.

### Cited Findings

- The "outdated or thin sites" thesis is only partly supported. For most 2026 changes found, several sites already had "2026" pages by September 2026. Gaps are more likely in the following, and are worth validating with a keyword tool:

## Q4. Practical considerations: AdSense language support, machine translation, URL structure, localization

### Takeaway
All target languages are AdSense-supported. Google does not treat AI or machine translation as spam in itself, but translated pages that add "little to no value" fall under the March 2024 scaled-content-abuse policy. Google recommends distinct URLs per language (subdirectories work) with hreflang, and warns against IP-based locale switching. Country tools need deep localization: regional tax rates, decimal-comma formats, currency, and legal rules. A literal translation of an English calculator will not produce a German Grunderwerbsteuer or French notary-fee tool.

### Cited Findings
- AdSense supports about 44 languages, including Arabic, Danish, Dutch, Finnish, French, German, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Norwegian, Polish, Portuguese, Spanish and a separate "Spanish (Latin American)", Swedish, Turkish and others. Sites may contain multiple supported languages. [Official page blocked; list via search summary of the official page and aggregators] — [Google AdSense Help: Languages Google publisher products support](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/9727?hl=en); [alltop9.com](https://www.alltop9.com/languages-google-adsense-supports/)
- Google stops showing ads on unsupported-language sites. — [MonetizeMore](https://www.monetizemore.com/blog/google-stops-showing-ads-unsupported-language-sites-non-content-pages/)
- Google's March 2024 "scaled content abuse" spam policy lists automated transformations, including translations, as potential violations when they produce content with "little to no value to users". — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-removes-robots-txt-guidance-for-blocking-auto-translated-pages/548870/); [gsqi.com (Glenn Gabe)](https://www.gsqi.com/marketing-blog/auto-translating-content-google-scaled-content-abuse/)
- In June 2025 Google told Glenn Gabe that its policies "do not strictly define content that has been translated by AI as spam", after Reddit's mass AI-translated pages were not penalized. — [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-softens-automated-translation-stance-39579.html); [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-comments-on-reddits-use-of-ai-to-translate-its-pages-456908); [Glenn Gabe on X](https://x.com/glenngabe/status/1932820373587763485)
- Google removed its old guidance recommending robots.txt blocking of auto-translated pages as outdated. Scaled-content-abuse policies judge value regardless of how content is produced. — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-removes-robots-txt-guidance-for-blocking-auto-translated-pages/548870/); [Slator](https://slator.com/google-flips-stance-on-automatic-ai-translations-requires-content-creators-to-opt-out/)
- Google recommends different URLs for each language version, not cookie or browser-setting switching, and hreflang or sitemaps to indicate alternates. It warns that IP-based locale adaptation is unreliable. - Evidence of localization depth that competitors provide:
  - Grunderwerbsteuer tools cover "alle 16 Bundesländer" (all 16 German states). — [immo-rechner.net](https://immo-rechner.net/ratgeber/grunderwerbsteuer-2026)
  - French notary tools calculate "par département". — [mesfraisnotaire.fr](https://www.mesfraisnotaire.fr/)
  - Spanish net-salary tools use exact IRPF rates "por CCAA" (per autonomous region). — [financialtools.web.app](https://financialtools.web.app/herramientas/sueldo-neto/)
  - Polish salary tools cover several contract types (umowa o pracę employment contract, zlecenie civil contract, B2B), ZUS/NFZ contributions and PPK pension savings. — [hrappka.pl](https://hrappka.pl/kalkulator-wynagrodzen/)

### Inferences
- **Machine translation:**
  - AI-translating universal calculators (percentage, age, units, compound interest) is acceptable if each page adds real value: a working tool, localized examples, correct number formats.
  - Mass-translating thin explanatory text at scale is the risk case.
  - A native review of the top pages is prudent, especially for YMYL finance, tax and legal tools.
- **URL and locale structure:**
  - Suggested structure: example.com/de/, /fr/, /ja/, with hreflang including x-default, and no automatic IP redirects.
  - Country-specific rule tools should live only in their own language folder, e.g. /de/grunderwerbsteuer-rechner/, not translated.
  - Where one language has several country rule sets, use a separate URL per country, e.g. /de-at/ for Austria's Brutto-Netto (AT has its own BMF calculator) and /fr-ch/ for Switzerland.
- **Formatting and localization:** decimal comma and thousands separators (1.234,56 € in DE; 1 234,56 € in FR), local currency, date formats, and local legal disclaimers. For EU markets, a TCF-certified consent management platform is required for AdSense personalized ads (general industry knowledge, not verified in this run).

### Gaps
- The exact current wording of Google's spam policy and the AdSense language list could not be read directly (pages blocked).
- AdSense's policy on pages that mix multiple languages was not confirmed.

## Q5. Go deep in one non-English market vs. broad across many languages

### Takeaway
No calculator-specific case study comparing "deep single market" with "broad multilingual" was found. The available evidence is indirect: Omni Calculator's early international traffic and translation program, and Preply's 50-language programmatic build. It suggests that translated universal tools can add traffic cheaply, while high-RPM, low-competition opportunities sit in country-specific rule calculators that need local depth. A hybrid is the likely best fit: one deep market plus translated universal tools. This is an inference, not a tested result.

### Cited Findings
- Omni Calculator (Kraków; bootstrapped with no VC funding) reported that about 30% of its traffic came from non-English-speaking countries. It said this "emphasises the need to go global rather than to focus on a specific region", and it planned translation into 15 languages. [OLDER interview, date not confirmed, likely before 2022; search summary only] — [omgkrk.com interview](https://www.omgkrk.com/omni_calculator_interview/)
- Omni's site says translations into Spanish, French, German, Italian and more are in progress. It reports 3,000-3,700+ calculators. — [omnicalculator.com About](https://www.omnicalculator.com/about-us); [Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.omnicalculator.app)
- Omni Calculator is featured as a Google for Publishers success story, and in a consentmanager case study about raising EU consent opt-in rates after a CMP switch. This shows EU consent rates matter to calculator-site yield. [Both pages blocked; titles only] — [Google for Publishers](https://www.google.com/ads/publisher/stories/omni_calculator/); [consentmanager](https://www.consentmanager.net/en/case-studies/omni-calculator/)
- Preply built about 60,000 pages in 50 languages, and organic traffic grew from about 500k to about 4M monthly over three years. [Vendor-published case; not a calculator site] — [junia.ai](https://www.junia.ai/blog/agency-case-study-programmatic-seo)
- A B2B SaaS site grew from about 3,500 to about 13,700 monthly visitors (+290%) in 5 months by translating into several languages. [Vendor case study, small scale] — [linkpanda.com](https://linkpanda.com/international-seo/)

### Inferences
- **Broad (universal tools in many languages):**
  - Cheap to scale, and the tools work with any currency.
  - They compete with multilingual incumbents (Omni Calculator, and in each market the local multi-tool portals listed in Q1/Q2).
  - Low-CPC utility queries produce low RPM.
  - Scaled-translation risk if pages are thin.
- **Deep (one market's rule calculators):**
  - Higher-intent finance, tax and legal queries.
  - Yearly rule churn creates fresh demand and a maintenance moat.
  - Needs native-level language skills, legal accuracy and YMYL trust signals.
- **Suggested path for a new site:**
  - Launch deep in one market, German or Japanese, with 10-20 rule calculators.
  - Add translated versions of the 10-20 strongest universal tools into 3-5 high-RPM languages under subdirectories with hreflang.
  - Expand deep rule tools to a second market (FR or IT) only once the first shows traction.

### Gaps
- No public revenue or traffic case study of a calculator site that picked one non-English market vs many languages.
- No AdSense RPM comparison between translated universal tools and native rule calculators.
- Omni Calculator's current language list and its per-language traffic were not verified (pages blocked, search budget exhausted).
